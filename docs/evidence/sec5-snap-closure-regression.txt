================================================================================
3. Mechanism
================================================================================

6dae34ea added a per-request time budget to the snap/1 server,
*SNAP-SYNC-SERVE-SECONDS* = 1 (src/networking/snap-sync/backend.lisp).
SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-RESPONSE asks the stop function after every slot; once
the second is spent it sets TRUNCATED-P, attaches the edge proof and ends the
response, even when the slot just pushed was the account's last one. snap/1
allows a server to answer any proved prefix, and the importer handles it
correctly: the account is resumed from the cut, so its storage has arrived in
two responses and it is treated as a chunked contract.

The fixture makes that cut happen. For a flat state,
SNAP-SYNC-ACCOUNT-STORAGE-TRIE finds each requested account with
SNAP-SYNC-FIND-ACCOUNT-ENTRY, a FIND over STATE-DB-ACCOUNT-RANGE (every
account of the state), and then rebuilds its storage trie. A StorageRanges
request that names a few hundred small contracts therefore costs a few
hundred whole-state scans, and in the cold container that is more than a
second. One small contract gets cut, is judged chunked, and in the
storage-closure-off arm nothing publishes its whole-root proof: the fourth
open verdict. Before 6dae34ea the server had no clock and the population was
three.

Measured with a temporary probe (deleted before commit) that wrapped
SNAP-SYNC-SERVE-STOP-FUNCTION to count true answers and ran the
storage-closure-off arm twice on 0af3bef7:

  budget 1 s (default)   serve-stops=1  open-verdicts=4  withheld=19 of 2,647
  budget NIL             serve-stops=0  open-verdicts=3  withheld=17 of 2,647

17 is the "measured 17 of 2,647" that the test's comment records.

================================================================================
4. Product or test?
================================================================================

Test. Nothing in the failure shows the closed writer or I1 going wrong:

- The production arm (storage closure on) ran first in every red run, under
  the same budget. In the 04a3aff4 and b4e46d96 runs it reported
  open-verdicts=0, withheld-open-storage=0 and i1-subtree-violations=0, the
  same as 4d51d222. The saved a6d2b58b line is cut off after open-verdicts=0.
  Whether a time cut also hit that arm was not counted (section 6). So the partitioned-storage
  closure step closes the cut contract before its account page resumes, as
  docs/snap-account-closure.md section 3.2 says it must.
- The fourth verdict appears only in the arm that switches that step off. It
  is the correct fail-closed result: the contract's storage came in two
  responses, nothing proved its root, and its path was withheld. Withholding
  is the safe direction.
- A server may cut a StorageRanges answer anywhere and prove the cut (geth
  cuts on its byte budget). The client's writer has to hold I1 for any such
  cut, not for one particular server timing.

What the assertion encoded incidentally is that the fixture's server cuts
only on the byte cap. After 6dae34ea that depends on the wall clock. The
fix keeps the pinned numbers and makes them a function of the fixture data
and the byte cap again. CALL-WITH-SNAP-DENSITY-WRITER, the fixture's global
seam, now sets *SNAP-SYNC-SERVE-SECONDS* to NIL for the run and restores it
in the same UNWIND-PROTECT. It sets rather than binds, like the seam's other
globals, because the multi-source importer serves on worker threads. The
fixture's backend has no yield predicate, so with the budget off its stop
function is always false. No assertion was loosened: (= 3 open-verdicts) and
every I1 assertion stay as they were.

6. Not verified
================================================================================

- No test cuts a small contract on the server's clock and then asserts I1 on
  the production arm. That evidence comes from the red runs above, where the
  cut happened by timing, and it is not pinned. Those runs also do not show
  whether the production arm's own run was cut: the probe counted stops only
  in the storage-closure-off arm.
- Other fixtures that serve a large flat state through
  MAKE-PERSISTENT-SNAP-STATE-BACKEND are exposed to the same budget. None of
  the 33 + 333 selected tests failed, but they were not audited one by one.
- The flat-state storage lookup is O(accounts) per requested account. That is
  a server cost on in-memory states only: SNAP-SYNC-ACCOUNT-STORAGE-TRIE skips
  it for lazy states. It was not changed.
- cold-all and the eight EEST gates were not run. No execution or state code
  changed.
