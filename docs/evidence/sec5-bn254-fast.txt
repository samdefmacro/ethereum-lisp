================================================================================
1. Design choice
================================================================================

The brief offered (A) routing 0x06-0x08 through "the existing Go helper on
vendored gnark-crypto" or (B) Montgomery field arithmetic in Lisp. (A) has no
base to extend: f930e9e5 ("Retire the Go-helper subprocess crypto backends",
2026-07-24) deleted the Go helpers and their vendored gnark-crypto tree;
EIP-2537 now runs in-process on blst through CFFI, and blst has no BN254.
A new native dependency (a C BN254 library behind CFFI) would be a new pinned
input in both Dockerfiles and tools/build-inputs/inputs.lock, which is a
supply-chain change outside this brief. So (B), with the integer code kept
as the oracle, which also keeps PROJECT.md's rule for hand-written crypto:
small, covered by the official vectors and a differential oracle.

The subgroup test and the scalar multiplication paid one modular inverse per
point addition; everything else paid bignum multiplication and division per
field operation.

================================================================================
3. Changes (4683a9e5)
================================================================================

bn254-montgomery.lisp: Fp over four little-endian 64-bit limbs in a word
vector at an offset, Montgomery form, always fully reduced (equal values,
equal limbs). CIOS multiplication with gnark-crypto's no-carry shortcut (p's
top limb is below 2^63 - 1); add, sub, neg with one conditional correction;
binary extended-Euclid inverse over plain limbs, then one Montgomery product
by R^3; canonical 32-byte decoding that refuses values >= p (geth's gnark
SetBytesCanonical). Nothing allocates a bignum.

bn254-tower.lisp: Fp2, Fp6 and Fp12 in word vectors with the integer
implementation's representations and formulas (Fp12 multiplication in
Karatsuba form), Frobenius constants converted once from its constants.

bn254-fast.lisp: Jacobian doubling and mixed addition for G1 and the twist
(infinity, P + P and P + (-P) handled explicitly), ECMUL by double-and-add
over the 32 scalar bytes, the G2 subgroup test as r*Q = infinity in Jacobian
coordinates (the oracle's test, no inverse), the Miller loop and final
exponentiation on the oracle's schedules, decoding and the three runners.
The runners keep the oracle's framing, validation order, failure messages,
dropping of pairs with a point at infinity and gas;
*BN254-PAIRING-CHECKER* now receives the decoded word-vector pairs.

bn254-base.lisp, bn254-pairing.lisp: the integer runners are renamed
RUN-BN254-{ADD,MUL,PAIRING}-PRECOMPILE-REFERENCE and kept as the oracle.

Three SBCL 2.2.9 arm64 findings shaped the code:

- The four-argument SB-BIGNUM:%MULTIPLY-AND-ADD VOP writes its low result
  before reading its addends. When the allocator gave that result the
  register of a dying addend, the addend was lost: the first version's
  Montgomery product of 2 and 3 was wrong, the disassembly showing
  "ADDS NL3, NL3, NL3" (the product added to itself). The carry helpers use
  only single-result operations: a modular product, SB-KERNEL:%MULTIPLY-HIGH,
  modular sums.
- With carries written as comparisons and the conditional corrections as
  branches, one Fp2 product cost 370 ns on evolving data against 90 ns when
  the same input was repeated, the signature of data-dependent branch
  mispredictions. Carries now come from the operands' top bits and the
  corrections select with masks: 175 ns on evolving data, the whole
  one-pair pairing 5.0 -> 2.5 ms.
- DYNAMIC-EXTENT on (make-array n :element-type '(unsigned-byte 64)) is
  ignored ("could not stack allocate"), so heap temporaries cost a pairing
  2.7 MB. Each runner binds one workspace (a special, so thread-local) and
  every routine takes named scratch slots above its top, with a bounds check
  that signals instead of overrunning: 15.5 KB consed per one-pair pairing.

ECADD stays inverse-bound (one binary-Euclid inverse, ~2.3 us). By the
component rows, a four-pair verifier call (four G2 decodes, four Miller

sec5-evm-throughput-2 brief puts go-ethereum's gnark path at about 1 ms per
pairing; the remaining gap is cyclotomic squaring, sparse line
multiplication and an endomorphism subgroup test, none of which is done
here (the oracle's schedules were kept so the tower is checkable routine by
routine).
