Fixture (tests/snap-closure-density-tests.lisp)

SNAP-DENSITY-STATE builds a state from a seeded 64-bit LCG, so every number
here is reproducible and no assertion depends on randomness. At 5,000
accounts: 767 contracts (15%) drawing code from a pool of one code per eight
contracts, 641 storage-owning accounts (12.8%; live Hoodi ranges showed about
9%, docs/validation.md), 6,982 slots. Most contracts own one to eight slots,
20% own none, and three own 1,000 to 2,000 slots. The byte cap is 30,000
bytes for AccountRange and StorageRanges alike (the importer sends one cap
for both); it yields 500 accounts per page on the single-source path and
chunks the three wide contracts. SNAP-DENSITY-REBASE changes each
account with probability 15 per mille (62 of 5,000 here: nonce and
balance, plus one storage slot for a contract), which moves 178 of the
6,873 account-trie nodes.

A rebase arm downloads the first half of the pages under the old root,
rebases the progress with SNAP-SYNC-REBASE-PROGRESS, finishes the range
under the new root and heals it, which is how
SNAP-MID-RANGE-REBASE-ZEROES-RANGE-PLAN-PROMOTION drives the live stall.

What is counted, and how

  Store faced by the healer: every account-trie node of the target root is
  looked up at SNAP-SYNC-HEAL-STATE entry (after the range phase and the
  deferred-storage fill).
  Withheld cause: a withheld node is charged to open storage when an open
  storage-owning leaf lies beneath it, else open code, else a leaf no page
  ever judged (changed by the rebase after its page downloaded), else the
  page-boundary straddle. "Open" is the closed writer's own predicate
  verdict, recorded by wrapping SNAP-SYNC-ACCOUNT-CLOSURE-PREDICATE.
  Healer account walk: the importer's heal counters mix account and storage
  nodes, so account nodes are split out at two seams. "reached" is the
  distinct account references the healer created work for
  (SNAP-SYNC-MAKE-HEAL-WORK), "proof skips" the distinct account references
  a closure proof answered (SNAP-SYNC-FILTERED-HEALED-SUBTREES-PRESENT),
  "decoded" = reached - proof skips.
  Presence-skip estimate: a walk from the root that fetches and descends
  every absent node and stops at every present one. It is valid only because
  the closed store holds I1, which the fixture reads back (a present node
  with an absent descendant counts as an I1 violation; zero in every closed
  arm, two in the legacy rebase arm at 2,000 accounts).

  The answer the coordinator asked for: with the closed writer plus an
  account presence skip, the post-rebase heal is estimated to touch 848
  account nodes and decode 131 of them, against 1,317 touched and 1,136
  decoded for the legacy healer at depth-one geometry, and 839 touched and
  199 decoded at depth-two geometry. The skip decodes fewer account nodes in
  both geometries; it touches about as many as legacy when legacy's range
  proofs happen to cover whole buckets (depth two), and 36% fewer when they
  do not (depth one). It fetches 28 more nodes than legacy, the price of not
  writing the spine.

  Which depth is live: at 4-nibble publication a live bucket holds hundreds
  of accounts and a live page thousands, so a page covers roughly ten
  buckets; the fixture's depth two (20 accounts a bucket, 25 buckets a page)
  and depth one (312 a bucket, 1.6 a page) bracket it.

Measurements: 2,000 accounts, multi-source importer (two sources)

  64 tasks at 2,000 accounts is 32 accounts a page, so this is the
  task-bounded shape, not page density. 65 pages, 2,647 nodes.

  No rebase, closed writer: withheld 24 (0.91%): 10 open storage, 14
  straddle. Heal decoded 24 account nodes with 277 proof skips at either
  depth, identical to the presence-skip estimate (301).
  Legacy: nothing decoded, 1,834 (depth 2) / 266 (depth 1) promoted.

  Rebase after 32 pages, 30 accounts / 88 nodes changed:
                              legacy             closed (current healer)
    depth 2 reached/decoded   390 / 65           465 / 203
    depth 1 reached/decoded   442 / 205          465 / 203
    presence-skip estimate                       413 touched, 60 fetched

  Here the estimate decodes slightly fewer than legacy (60 vs 65) but touches
  more (413 vs 390) at depth two. Small pages already keep every maximal
  closed root deep, so the closed writer's own proofs do most of the work.

  Open code was 0 in every arm: code was durable by page completion on both
  importers, so the cross-thread code seam did not hold an account open here.

Findings

  1. The withheld fraction at page density is under one percent (0.49% at
     500 accounts a page, 0.64% at 400 in the pinned test) and is dominated
     by the chunked contracts and the page-boundary spine. Denser pages only
     shrink the straddle share.

  2. The closed writer must not ship without the presence skip. With the
     healer in this tree, a closed store heals worse than legacy after a
     rebase (2,276-3,036 decoded against 199-1,136), and even without a
     rebase at depth two (2,532 decoded against 0). The healer trusts an
     account node only through a closure proof at its lookup depth, and
     after a rebase the nodes at that depth above every changed path are new
     hashes. This is the interim state the seam exists for; it is now pinned
     as a defect assertion so the presence-skip change has to reverse it.

  3. Closure proofs are published only for maximal closed roots at or below
     the publication depth (SNAP-SYNC-CLOSED-ACCOUNT-PAGE-CONTENT). A
     maximal root shallower than that depth publishes nothing for its
     descendants, so a healer that looks only at the lookup depth walks the
     whole subtree: 2,532 decoded at depth two against 34 at depth one on the
     same store. A presence skip at any depth makes this moot; a skip
     restricted to the lookup depth would not.

  4. The brief's "about 470 accounts per page" is not what the repo's own
     live evidence shows: docs/validation.md records Hoodi range runs of
     2,336,962 accounts in 251 pages and 587,492 in 82 (about 5,600 to 9,300
     accounts a page at the 512 KiB request size). The fixture uses 400-500
     so that several pages exist at a size the dev image can hold.

The pinned test

SNAP-CLOSED-ACCOUNT-WRITER-AT-PAGE-DENSITY-AND-AFTER-A-MID-RANGE-REBASE
(integration, 2,000 accounts, depth one, single source, about 21 s warm):

  no rebase, closed:   400 accounts/page, 17 of 2,647 withheld (10 open
                       storage, 7 straddle), 3 open accounts, 0 I1
                       violations; asserts withheld < 1% and both causes
                       non-zero.
  rebase, legacy:      reached 417, decoded 259, 2 I1 violations.
  rebase, closed:      presence-skip estimate 366 touched / 64 fetched;
                       current healer decoded 947; 0 I1 violations.
  asserts 64 < 259 and 366 < 417, and (defect) 947 > 259.

RED controls

  The withheld bound: SNAP-DENSITY-CLASSIFY over a store that holds nothing
  reports every node withheld, which fails the one-percent bound; asserted
  in the test.
  The I1 counter: a store missing one leaf under present ancestors reports a
  violation; asserted in the test. The legacy rebase arm, which persists the
  spine above changed paths, reports 2 violations, so the closed arm's zero
  is a discriminating check and not an idle counter.
  The comparison: the legacy arm is the positive control -- it must decode
  more than three times the changed nodes the closed arm reports as
  unjudged (259 > 3 x 48), so the healer the estimate is compared with
  really re-walks.

Measured with the real skip

Revision: branch closure-density-real on 5605c936, which carries the
epoch-seven change (closure writes on by default, scheme #(7), and
INTEGRATE-PRESENT-WORK skipping a present unmarked account node on a store
whose closed-account scheme is on). Same fixture, same byte cap, same
rebase. The estimate above is replaced by the healer itself.

The two arms now differ by the store, not by a flag. The closed arm is a
fresh store, which enters epoch seven: closed writer plus the presence skip.
The legacy arm is seeded by SNAP-TEST-SEED-LEGACY-TRIE-STORE with one trie
node before the stamp, so it never enters the epoch: legacy writer, no
account presence skip and, unlike the earlier legacy arm, no storage
presence skip either. That is why the legacy totals below carry about 2,300
more processed nodes than before; they are storage nodes, and the account
columns are the comparison.

Account columns, split out of the healer's mixed counters at two seams:
touched = distinct account references the healer created work for
(SNAP-SYNC-MAKE-HEAL-WORK); expanded = account nodes it descended into (an
interior node whose children it pushed, a leaf whose account it decoded
while healing); fetched = touched nodes absent from the store at
SNAP-SYNC-HEAL-STATE entry. The earlier "decoded" (reached minus closure-proof
skips) is retired: a presence skip is neither.

Single-source importer, mid-range rebase, touched / expanded / fetched:

                                legacy store          closed epoch-7 store
  2,000 accounts, 88 of 2,647 nodes changed, rebase after 2 of 5 pages
    lookup depth 1              417 / 257 / 50        366 / 64 / 64
    lookup depth 2              432 /  79 / 50        366 / 64 / 64
  5,000 accounts, 178 of 6,873 nodes changed, rebase after 5 of 10 pages
    lookup depth 1            1,317 / 1,133 / 103     848 / 131 / 131
    lookup depth 2              839 /   196 / 103     848 / 131 / 131

  The real healer on the closed store reproduces the estimate exactly
  (366 / 64 and 848 / 131): it expands precisely the nodes it fetches.

  No rebase, closed store: 2,000 accounts 156 / 17 / 17, 5,000 accounts
  344 / 34 / 34 at both depths. The legacy store expands no account node
  without a rebase (its range proofs cover the trie).

What this establishes

  The closed store expands strictly fewer account nodes than the legacy
  store in all four rebase cells: 64 against 257 and 79, 131 against 1,133
  and 196. The ratio is widest where a lookup bucket is wider than a page
  (depth one) and narrowest where legacy range proofs cover whole buckets
  (depth two).

  It touches fewer in three cells and 9 more (848 against 839, +1.1%) in
  the fourth, 5,000 accounts at depth two. The skip stops at the present
  siblings of every changed path, and legacy's depth-two range proofs stop
  at about as many. Touched counts one lookup per node, not one expansion.

  It fetches more in every cell (64 against 50, 131 against 103): the spine
  and page-boundary nodes the closed writer never writes and the open
  chunked-storage paths. That is the design's stated price.
