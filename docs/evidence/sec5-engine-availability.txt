Section 5: Engine availability under long store-guard holds

Status

Branch engine-availability, from a07c912e (b5161312 + docs). The R4 Hoodi
run (container hoodi-el-sec5-b5161312, Lighthouse hoodi-lighthouse-public)
lost its consensus client from 01:27Z: engine.rpc.http.connection.error
"HTTP request exceeded the 30 second deadline" 4-32 times a minute, no
newPayload or forkchoiceUpdated completed after 01:50:27Z, and Lighthouse
logged "Error during exchange capabilities ... timeout" every few seconds.

Found, reproduced and fixed on the Engine side:
  (c) engine_exchangeCapabilities and engine_getClientVersionV1 took the
      store guard, although they read no store state;
  (a) a request parked on the guard keeps its connection's HTTP worker slot
      until the 30 s deadline, so parked requests also keep new connections
      out of the 32-slot pool;
  and nothing bounded how long newPayload / forkchoiceUpdated wait behind a
  background hold.
Also: the forward batch importer held two slow blocks per hold, and long_hold
now names the activity and the blocks of every hold. Not deployed, no live
run, no cold-all (the coordinator runs it once at the end).

================================================================================
2. Mechanism (question 1 of the brief)
================================================================================

(c) The Engine service's guard predicate (src/app/cli/devnet/node.lisp)
    exempted only eth_syncing and engine_getBlobsV3. RPC-HANDLE-REQUEST
    therefore ran engine_exchangeCapabilities and engine_getClientVersionV1
    under the Engine priority guard, behind any background hold. Both only
    return static data (ENGINE-RPC-CAPABILITIES, ENGINE-RPC-CLIENT-VERSION).
    This is why a call that needs no state timed out: confirmed by the
    reproduction below (3705 ms behind a 4 s hold).
(a) ENGINE-RPC-HTTP-SERVICE-SERVE-LISTENER takes a worker slot per accepted
    connection and returns it when the connection ends. A request parked on
    the guard keeps the slot until the guard frees or the 30 s deadline
    fires; the CL has long since given up (8 s, 1 s for metadata) and opened
    a new connection for its next call. With the pool full the accept loop
    blocks, and a new connection waits in the listen backlog. Reproduced
    with a four-slot pool: a capabilities call waited 2310 ms for a slot.
    Live, 25 connections a minute each parked 30 s means about 12 of the 32
    slots on average, so (a) is a secondary, burst-time effect; (c) and the
    unbounded wait are the primary ones.
(b) Not the cause: each connection has its own worker, and every CL call
    that timed out arrived on its own connection (one stream.start /
    stream.finish pair per deadline error). The JWT check is pure (HMAC in
    src/transport/http/auth.lisp) and takes no guard.

================================================================================
3. Fix
================================================================================

a. Metadata calls without the guard. *DEVNET-ENGINE-GUARD-FREE-METHODS* adds
   engine_exchangeCapabilities and engine_getClientVersionV1 to eth_syncing
   and engine_getBlobsV3. Pinned go-ethereum (38271784, 1.17.6-unstable,
   eth/catalyst/api.go ExchangeCapabilities, GetClientVersionV1) takes no
   chain lock for either.

b. Bounded Engine wait behind background holds. *DEVNET-ENGINE-GUARD-BUSY-
   SECONDS* = 7. The priority guard waits in 0.1 s slices and counts only
   the time during which the owner is NOT an Engine request (the ledger now
   records HOLDER-ENGINE-P); waiting behind another Engine request is the
   CL's own serialization, which geth also imposes (newPayloadLock,
   forkchoiceLock), and stays unbounded. When the budget runs out it
   accounts the wait (guardWaitMs, guardWaitedFor ending in
   "LABEL:HELDms(holding)[blocks=N]") and signals
   ETHEREUM-LISP.RPC:RPC-REQUEST-GUARD-BUSY; RPC-HANDLE-REQUEST answers
   ENGINE-RPC-STORE-BUSY-RESPONSE:
     engine_newPayloadV*          {status: SYNCING, latestValidHash: null}
     engine_forkchoiceUpdatedV*   {payloadStatus: SYNCING, payloadId: null}
     other guarded Engine calls   -32000 "execution client busy ..."
   Why 7 s: the execution-apis Engine timeouts (engine/common.md) are 8 s
   for newPayload and forkchoiceUpdated, and Lighthouse's log shows exactly
   that 8-9 s cadence; a normal one-block hold on Hoodi costs about 6 s, so
   7 s lets it finish, and the SYNCING still reaches the CL before its
   deadline. geth parity: geth answers SYNCING when it cannot take a payload
   yet (delayPayloadImport, "Ignoring payload while snap syncing") or a
   forkchoice head it must sync to first (STATUS_SYNCING after BeaconSync);
   for a known head it blocks on its chain mutex. Giving up on a known head
   is ours, because our guard can be held by background work for minutes.
   The give-up does not buffer the payload (geth's remoteBlocks.put has no
   counterpart here); the CL resends on the next slot.
   Slot consequence: a parked request now leaves after at most ~7 s of
   background waiting, so the pool drains at 32 slots / 7 s; the worst case
   for a metadata call arriving when all 32 slots are parked is one budget.

c. Forward batch importer: one slow block per hold. The chunk decides before
   each block whether it is the last (only the last carries the durable
   export), and at b5161312 it looked only at the time already spent, so
   with 6 s blocks and a 1 s budget every hold ran two blocks when no Engine
   request happened to be waiting. It now also ends the hold when this block
   plus one more at the latest block's measured cost would reach the budget
   (*DEVNET-PEER-SYNC-LAST-BLOCK-TICKS*). What a single hold still costs:
   one block execution (plus that block's durable export), except the very
   first hold of the process, which has no measured cost and may run two.
   The gap filler already holds one block per hold (DEVNET-PEER-SYNC-IMPORT-
   BLOCK), and every non-Engine taker defers to a waiting Engine request
   (6fe683b0); unchanged.

d. Every hold says what it was doing. CALL-WITH-DEVNET-NODE-STORE-GUARD-AS
   names the hold; the peer-session takers are now labelled eth-serve-block,
   tx-pool-lookup, tx-admission, blob-sidecar-admission, blob-sidecar-lookup,
   tx-broadcast-scan, peer-block-import, snap-serve-open,
   snap-serve-account-range, snap-serve-storage-ranges, snap-serve-bytecodes,
   snap-serve-trie-nodes; the coordinator's per-block imports
   forward-block-import, engine-target-import, snap-tail-import.
   DEVNET-PEER-SYNC-IMPORT-BLOCK-WITHOUT-GUARD notes each block number in the
   hold (DEVNET-STORE-GUARD-NOTE-BLOCK), and node.store_guard.long_hold gains
   a "blocks" field ("N", "FIRST..LAST(COUNT)" or "none"). A long hold of
   one block is P's (block cost); a hold with blocks "none" is a server or
   admission hold; a hold of several blocks is a batching defect.

e. Gap filler vs the CL's own newPayload (brief item 4): not changed. The
   R4 log has zero gap_filled / head_filled events, so there is no evidence
   that it competed with Engine in this run; the aee866f7 case (144 gap
   fills) is already bounded to one block per hold with Engine priority,
   and a newPayload whose block the filler already imported answers VALID
   without execution.

Not verified
================================================================================

- Nothing was deployed. Whether the next run's CL stays online depends on
  the Engine answering SYNCING within 8 s, which the reproduction shows
  locally, not live.
- The identity of the 20-134 s dial-session holds and of the hold in
  progress from 01:50Z: the new labels will name them. The cgroup data
  (memory at its 12 GiB limit, 190 MB page cache, 57% I/O pressure, then an
  OOM kill) points at memory, not at the guard; out of scope here.
- A SYNCING fallback does not buffer the payload or record the forkchoice
  target; while a background hold outlasts the budget on every slot the node
  learns no new CL target. The previous target is still being worked on by
  the importer that holds the guard.
- A metadata call that finds all 32 slots parked still waits up to one
  budget (7 s) for a slot, longer than the CL's 1 s; the pool drains at
  32 slots per 7 s, well above Lighthouse's observed request rate.
- Other guarded Engine calls (getPayload, getPayloadBodies, getBlobsV1/V2)
  now fail with -32000 after the budget instead of the 30 s deadline; the
  CL's own deadlines for them (1-10 s) are shorter, so it sees a timeout
  either way.
- Hive and EEST were not rerun: no execution, root or receipt code changed;
  an Engine request behind another Engine request is not bounded, so Hive's
  serialized Engine traffic keeps its behaviour.
