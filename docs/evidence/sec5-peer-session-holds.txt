Section 5: peer-session store-guard holds and the post-01:50Z heap growth

Status

Branch peer-session-holds, from 886afd05. The R4 Hoodi run (container
hoodi-el-sec5-b5161312) showed peer-session store-guard holds of 20-134 s
from 00:26Z, and from 01:50:17Z one hold that never ended while the Lisp heap
grew from ~0.5 GB to 3.95 GB until the OOM kill at 02:25:29Z.

Found, reproduced and fixed: the snap/1 server answered GetAccountRange and
GetStorageRanges through MPT-ENTRY-RANGE, which enumerates and sorts EVERY
leaf of a lazily persisted trie before it looks at the request's origin,
limit or byte budget, and it resolved those nodes into the served state's own
(shared, retained) node graph. One request therefore read and decoded the
whole account trie (or a contract's whole storage trie) under the store
guard, and kept about 1.4 KB per account in the heap afterwards. Serving now
walks from the origin and stops at the limit key, the byte budget, a 1 s
per-request time budget, or as soon as an Engine request waits, answering the
proved prefix snap/1 allows; resolutions are per-request, never memoized into
the served state. GetByteCodes and GetTrieNodes gained geth's lookup and
soft-byte caps, and inbound transaction batches are admitted 64 per hold.
Not deployed, no live run, no cold-all (the coordinator runs it once).

================================================================================
2. Mechanism
================================================================================

(a) Unbounded range walks. SNAP-SYNC-ACCOUNT-RESPONSE and SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-
    RESPONSE (src/networking/snap-sync/backend.lisp at 886afd05) called
    (MPT-ENTRY-RANGE TRIE :START ORIGIN). For a lazy trie (MPT-LAZY-P: every
    trie the RocksDB direct provider serves) MPT-ENTRY-PAIRS walks the whole
    tree with TRIE-COLLECT-ENTRY-PAIRS, conses every (key . value), sorts the
    list by a hex string per key, and only then MPT-ENTRY-RANGE filters by
    START; the byte budget and Limit were applied to that list afterwards.
    The work of one GetAccountRange was the whole account trie, whatever the
    peer asked for. geth v1.17.x ServiceGetAccountRangeQuery and
    ServiceGetStorageRangesQuery (eth/protocols/snap/handler.go) iterate a
    flat snapshot from Origin and stop at Limit or once the response passes
    Bytes (capped at softResponseLimit = 2 MiB): their work is the response.
(b) Retention. TRIE-RESOLVE-NODE memoizes every resolved node in its
    HASH-NODE. The served trie is COPY-MPT of the state's trie, which shares
    the node graph -- with the chain store's committed tries for that block
    (CHAIN-STORE-STATE-DB copies (FIRST COMMITTED-TRIES)) and with every later
    state built from it. The backend also pins the session-open head state
    and a per-session cache of every root a peer asked for
    (DEVNET-NODE-SNAP-STATE-PROVIDER). So everything a walk resolved stayed
    in the heap: a full walk left ~1.4 KB per account resident, and even
    bounded requests from a peer syncing the whole key space would, over
    time, pin the whole decoded trie.
(c) Other kinds. GetByteCodes had no maxCodeLookups (1024) cap and no soft
    byte cap; GetTrieNodes had no soft byte cap and no clock (geth:
    maxTrieNodeLookups 1024, maxTrieNodeTimeSpent 5 s, softResponseLimit).
    Their work was still bounded (1024 lookups, request-sized hashes).
(d) Nothing inside a serving hold looked at the Engine priority signal; the
    forward batch importer does (e313f198). Transaction admission ran a whole
    wire batch (one sender recovery per transaction) under one hold.

What explains the live observations, and how firmly:
  - The hold from ~01:50:20Z that never ended, with page-cache reads and a
    steadily RETAINED heap climb: one GetAccountRange (or GetStorageRanges on
    a large contract) walking the whole trie under the guard, memoizing as it
    went. Measured locally (section 4): 1,425-1,463 B retained per account,
    plus ~1.6 KB per account live for the entry list until the walk ends.
    3.5 GB of growth is on the order of 1.1-1.2 million accounts walked; at
    the observed ~60 MB/min that is ~700 accounts a second, a plausible rate
    for RocksDB point reads from a cgroup with 190 MB of page cache left. The
    Hoodi account count was not measured, so "the walk would have needed more
    than the 12 GiB limit" is an inference, not a measurement.
  - The 20-134 s holds that did end: walks that finished (storage walks of
    large contracts, or account walks while the page cache was still warm),
    or bounded trie-node requests on a cold cache. Not attributable per hold
    from the b5161312 log (no labels, no per-request event).
  - Not eth serving: every eth lookup is its own hold of one block
    (+ETH-MAX-HEADERS/BODIES/RECEIPTS-SERVE+, 2 MiB soft limit), and the gap
    filler / forward importer carry their own labels (none in R4).

================================================================================
3. Fix
================================================================================

a. MPT-MAP-ENTRIES-FROM (src/foundation/trie/persistence.lisp): an ordered
   walk from START that resolves only START's path and the entries it visits
   and stops when the caller's function returns true. The account and
   storage handlers walk with it and stop at: the first key at or past Limit
   (included, as geth does), the byte budget (min Bytes 2 MiB), the time
   budget, or the yield predicate -- always after at least one item, so the
   requester progresses. The origin and last-key proofs are unchanged, so a
   cut range verifies as a partial range. Storage: a new account's range is
   not opened once the byte or time budget is spent (geth: "size >=
   req.Bytes"), and a cut inside an account is proved like a byte cut.
b. *TRIE-TRANSIENT-RESOLUTIONS* (src/foundation/trie/nodes.lisp): while bound
   to a table, TRIE-RESOLVE-NODE keeps resolutions in that table only. Each
   served request binds a fresh one (SNAP-SYNC-CALL-SERVING), so nothing a
   peer asks for stays in the served state's graph.
c. *SNAP-SYNC-SERVE-SECONDS* = 1 (our policy): per-request time budget for
   all four kinds. MAKE-PERSISTENT-SNAP-STATE-BACKEND takes :YIELD-PREDICATE;
   DEVNET-PEER-SNAP-BACKEND passes DEVNET-NODE-STORE-GUARD-PRIORITY-PENDING-P,
   so a serving hold ends within one item of an Engine request arriving.
d. geth caps: GetByteCodes serves at most 1024 hashes (maxCodeLookups) and
   caps Bytes at 2 MiB; GetTrieNodes caps Bytes at 2 MiB (the 1024-lookup cap
   already existed). Unknown roots still answer empty.
e. A lazy (direct) state's in-memory objects are no longer scanned for a
   flat storage entry on every requested account (O(objects) per account);
   only a flat, non-lazy state serves storage from memory.
f. Transaction admission: a wire batch is admitted in chunks of 64
   (+DEVNET-TX-ADMISSION-CHUNK+), one "tx-admission" hold each; the guard's
   Engine deferral (6fe683b0) runs between chunks.

Unknown-root, empty-root and cardinality behaviour is unchanged (the older
SNAP-SERVER/SNAP-BACKEND/SNAP-TRIE-NODE-SERVER tests are in the runs below).

Not verified

- Nothing was deployed. That the post-01:50Z hold was a full account (or
  storage) walk is inferred from the code path, the retained-heap slope and
  the page-cache reads; the b5161312 log has no per-request serving event.
  The Hoodi account count was not measured.
- Hive devp2p snap suite (6 tests, geth cmd/devp2p/internal/ethtest/snap.go)
  was not run. Expected unchanged: its state is small, so neither the 1 s
  budget nor the Engine yield fires, and Limit/Bytes accounting and proofs
  are unchanged. Two deliberate geth alignments could move a case: at most
  1024 bytecodes per request, and a storage request whose byte budget is
  spent no longer opens (and proves one slot of) the next account.
- A time- or yield-cut response is smaller than the peer asked for; geth's
  syncer treats a proved partial range as progress and re-asks from the last
  key. A peer that marks us slow for short answers would prefer other
  sources; not measured.
- eth serving and blob admission were read, not changed (one block or one
  transaction per hold). The per-session DEVNET-NODE-SNAP-STATE-PROVIDER
  cache still pins every state root a peer resolved for the session's life
  (small objects now that nothing is memoized into them).
- EEST/Hive consensus fixtures were not rerun: no execution, root or receipt
  code changed; the trie change is read-only walking and an opt-in
  non-memoizing resolution.
