Section 5 same-host geth control baseline

Purpose

Separate "our client is slow" from "public Hoodi snap peers are slow" by running
the pinned geth v1.17.4 control on the same host, in the same hour, against the
same public peer population, from an empty datadir.

Subject under test

ethereum-lisp revision fb4cb6f351c9ff68e048c78aa356d6dcaf6af8fc, container
hoodi-el-sec5-fb4cb6f3, fresh datadir
/data/hoodi-sec5-20260814/datadir-fb4cb6f3, started 2026-09-14T03:43:26Z,
12 GiB memory bound, non-root, read-only root, all capabilities dropped.

Control

geth v1.17.4, image ID
sha256:9389d3371a5cde510edb5dfa10a759f7ef98bd8676e6491ce79ab1050306478b,
container hoodi-geth-control-20260914, empty datadir
/data/hoodi-sec5-20260814/geth-control-20260914, started 2026-09-14T06:02:36Z

hoodi-el-public-36a22e47, the same JWT, the same hoodi-frozen/hoodi-net

dropped, no-new-privileges. The ethereum-lisp container was stopped for the

Host

test-ethereum-server (10-7-47-31), 8 vCPU AMD EPYC, 15 GiB RAM, Linux
5.15.0-112-generic. Data filesystem measured at 0.182 ms random 4 KiB O_DIRECT
reads (about 5,500 IOPS at queue depth one) and 718 MB/s sequential. Host
network was near idle throughout (534 KiB/s receive, 305 KiB/s transmit
measured during the ethereum-lisp run) and load average was 1.34.

ethereum-lisp over the same window

    elapsed   datadir      peers   snap sources   account ranges complete
    15m         0.15 GiB     11         -          0 of 64
    2h00m       4.57 GiB     13         9          0 of 64

Sustained rate: roughly 0.65 MB/s against geth's roughly 25.8 MB/s over its
first fifteen minutes and about 21 MB/s averaged to completion. The gap is
approximately fortyfold at comparable peer counts.

Conclusion

Public Hoodi snap peers served a complete state and chain download to a pinned
reference client in 78 minutes on this exact host and hour. Peer availability,
host CPU, host memory, disk latency, and host network are therefore excluded as
causes of the ethereum-lisp range-download stall. The bottleneck is in our
client.

Secondary observations

geth logged "Unexpected storage ranges packet" and "Expired request does not
exist" repeatedly throughout its run, discarded those responses, and continued
without retiring the peer. Our run classified 1,972 comparable events as
peer.snap.dependency_failed, 571 of them as an unclassified reason, over a much
smaller volume of work. Whether our handling of a late or expired response is
materially more costly than geth's is an open question for the range-download
audit, not a conclusion of this control.

Our per-peer telemetry during the stall recorded accountRttMs 40558,
accountTimeoutMs 60000, accountCap 524288, storageCap 65536, and a pool-wide
requestTimeoutMs of 8618, while dependency failures were dominated by 716
expiries at the 6.00 second floor.

One parity question was raised and closed during this investigation: geth does
size request capacity from its request timeout rather than from a raw
round-trip target. eth/protocols/snap/sync.go at the vendored commit
38271784c2b31926563806da9a2e023b88f5e7a8 computes
`targetTTL := s.rates.TargetTimeout()` and then
`s.rates.Capacity(id, kind, targetTTL)` at lines 1029-1035, 1126-1132,
1229-1235, 1386-1392, and 1514-1520. Our matching behaviour is correct and is
not the defect.

Scope

This control establishes a baseline and excludes environmental causes. It does
not identify the defect, and it does not bear on the separately fixed healer
dependency-deferral loop recorded in
docs/evidence/sec5-c0cce53d-heal-deferral-frontier.txt, which is in the final
healing phase that this run never reached.

Disposition

The geth control container is preserved stopped with its datadir intact at
/data/hoodi-sec5-20260814/geth-control-20260914 (98.79 GiB). The ethereum-lisp
container was restored at 2026-09-14T07:21:20Z and is running on its own
preserved datadir. Data filesystem utilisation after the control was 70% with
204 GiB available.
