3. engine-cancun "Parallel Blob Transactions": the builder spent its whole
   deadline asking whether blobs were held
================================================================================

What Hive sent and got: ten accounts sent 50 blob transactions of 6 blobs each
in parallel; all 50 answered with their hash. fcU V3 with attributes answered
VALID with a payload id; getPayloadV3 returned no transaction and an empty
blobs bundle. Hive: "Error verifying blob bundle (payload 1/1): expected 6
blob, got 0". Client log client-3bf4d47c...log: node.store_guard.long_hold
holder ethereum-lisp-devnet-payload-improvement holdMs 1080, then holder
engine_getPayloadV3 holdMs 1052 (handlerMs 1136).

Mechanism: before selecting, the builder keeps only the blob transactions
whose blobs this node holds (ENGINE-RPC-BUILDABLE-TRANSACTIONS, added by
f35bc82c). It asked ENGINE-PAYLOAD-STORE-BLOB-AND-PROOFS-V1 once per blob, and
every call enforced the blob cache bounds, which walks the whole cache, reads
each entry's pin, and sorts the keys twice (7eaba543 added the pin walk and
the second sort). 300 reads over a 300-entry cache: 1.2 s, before the stop
predicate is polled for the first candidate. The improvement pass (1 s bound)
and getPayload's own rebuild (0.3 s bound) both stopped before any candidate,
and the empty fcU payload was the one returned.

Reproduction, warm image, a Cancun devnet node with the same pool (50 x
6-blob transactions from 10 senders, admitted through the production blob
admission with stubbed proofs), fcU V3, one improvement pass, getPayloadV3:
  at 3c0cfeab: buildable filter 1,232 ms and 552 enforcements; pass 1,201 ms;
               getPayload 1,230 ms; 0 transactions, 0 blobs (Hive's answer).
  fixed:       buildable filter 4 ms; pass 10 ms; getPayload 9 ms;
               1 transaction, 6 blobs (Hive's expectation).

Fix: a blob read batch (CALL-WITH-ENGINE-PAYLOAD-STORE-BLOB-READ-BATCH,
src/storage/chain-store/service/cache.lisp) enforces the bounds once; reads
inside it on the same thread do not enforce again. The builder's filter uses a
non-copying presence read (ENGINE-PAYLOAD-STORE-BLOB-AVAILABLE-P) inside one
batch; the blobs bundle, getBlobsV1/V2/V3 (the snapshot reader too),
engine_hasBlobs and the eth pooled-sidecar read use one batch each. A read
outside a batch behaves as before.

Test: ENGINE-PAYLOAD-BUILD-ENFORCES-THE-BLOB-CACHE-BOUNDS-ONCE-PER-PASS
(tests/engine-payload-building-tests.lisp, integration). The Hive pool; the
filter keeps all 50 candidates with exactly one enforcement; then fcU V3, one
improvement pass and getPayloadV3 return one transaction and six blobs.

4. engine-cancun "Blob Transaction Ordering, Multiple Clients": the producer's
   session spent seconds deriving cell proofs and read nothing from its peer
================================================================================

What Hive sent and got: client A (fe1892f5, payload producer) and client B
(179b7551, dialled A over eth/72, not in the CL mock, left at genesis). Step 3
sent A five 5-blob transactions (blob fee cap 120), step 4 sent B five 1-blob
transactions (cap 100), step 5 asked A for a payload: getPayloadV3 returned
ONE transaction with 5 blobs (blobGasUsed 0xa0000). Hive expected 6, the
5-blob transaction plus one of B's, which reach A only through B. A's
getPayloadV3 took 36 ms and its selection is sound (the single-client
"Multiple Accounts" ordering case passed), so B's transactions were not in A's
pool. Neither session failed until the test ended (B logs peer.dial.failed,
Connection reset by peer, when A was torn down).

Mechanism, from a reproduction with two real devnet nodes over loopback RLPx
(eth/72), c-kzg cell and blob-proof computation real, cell and blob proof
verification timed on a real zero-blob verification and then answered true,
since the probe's blobs are distinct but their commitments are made up:
- Announcing a pooled blob transaction to an eth/72 peer built the full
  cell-proof wrapper to learn its size (ETH-PEER-ANNOUNCE-TRANSACTIONS through
  the eth/72 sidecar reader): 128 cell proofs per blob, 167 ms per blob here.
  A's session announced its own 5-blob transactions in calls of 942, 1,741 and
  1,585 ms, one after another on its writer thread.
- The same writer is the only reader of B's messages, so B's announcements of
  its own transactions, and B's GetCells for A's transactions, waited behind
  that work (B's GetCells for A's first transaction took 1,730 ms).
- Serving the wrapper then derived each blob in turn as well.
Schedule of the Hive step order (A admits a 5-blob transaction every 0.3 s,
then B a 1-blob transaction every 0.1 s), time from B's last admission until A
holds all five of B's transactions:
  at the base of this fix (sections 3 and 5 applied): 2.3 s, first arrival
    1.8 s; with A's five admissions back to back: 4.6 s, nothing for 3.5 s.
  fixed: 1.0 s; A's announcements take 0-1 ms and each 5-blob wrapper it
    serves 170-200 ms.
Hive asks for the payload about two seconds after step 4 (GetPayloadDelay 2),
so the unfixed schedule has no margin on a loaded host, and the failure is the
one Hive recorded.

Fix:
- src/networking/eth-sync/gossip.lisp: an eth/72 announcement is sized from
  the stored sidecar with 128 placeholder proofs per blob
  (ETH-ANNOUNCEMENT-CELL-PROOF-SIDECAR): every KZG proof is 48 bytes, so the
  size is the served wrapper's without deriving a proof. Used when cell proofs
  can be derived for serving; otherwise the previous reader decides.
- src/app/cli/devnet/peer-sync.lisp: a served wrapper derives its blobs'
  missing cells and proofs in parallel into the node's cell cache
  (DEVNET-NODE-DERIVE-BLOB-CELLS, at most 8 at once, helpers wrapped in
  HANDLER-CASE); c-kzg reads only its settings. GetCells then reads the cache.

Tests (tests/eth-gossip-tests.lisp, integration):
  ETH-72-BLOB-ANNOUNCEMENT-DERIVES-NO-CELL-PROOF: a pooled 2-blob transaction
    announced to an eth/72 peer through the production backend performs no
    derivation, and its announced size equals the served wrapper's
    announcement size; positive control: serving that wrapper derives both.
  ETH-72-BLOB-WRAPPER-DERIVES-ITS-BLOBS-IN-PARALLEL: serving a 5-blob wrapper
    derives each blob once, more than one at a time.
Existing ETH-72-NEW-POOLED-BLOB-ANNOUNCEMENT-USES-OMITTED-BLOB-SIZE and
ETH-72-BLOB-SERVING-DERIVES-CELLS-ONCE-AND-OUTSIDE-THE-STORE-GUARD still pass.

Not claimed: that this case passed at b5161312 for a reason this record
isolates. The announcement cost existed there too; the reproduction shows the
mechanism and its removal, the Hive rerun decides.

================================================================================
5. devp2p eth "BlobTxWithInvalidCells": the pool refused the transaction before
   the cells were checked
================================================================================

Pinned test (go-ethereum 101035a1 cmd/devp2p/internal/ethtest/suite.go
TestBlobTxWithInvalidCells): two peers announce one blob transaction; the peer
the client asks for its cells answers with zero cells; that peer must be
disconnected within readUntilDisconnect's 100 ms, the other must not. Hive
result: "expected peer to be disconnected after invalid cells". Client log
client-bff99672...log: both final sessions ended "RLPx stream ended" when the
suite hung up; no protocol error was raised.

Mechanism: makeBlobTxs takes sender 5 at its chain nonce, so the transaction
has the same sender and nonce as the one TestGetCells pooled just before it,
with the same fees. Since 7eaba543 (bd7906c3) our blob admission ran the
pool's policy checks first, refused the equal-fee replacement ("Blob
transaction replacement underpriced") as an ordinary pool refusal, and never
verified the cells; an ordinary refusal is skipped without charging the peer.
Before it, gossip verified the sidecar before offering the transaction to the
pool, which is why the case passed at d203fee6. go-ethereum v1.17.6
core/txpool/blobpool BlobPool.Add runs ValidateTxBasics and ValidateCells
before AddPooledTx and validateTx read any pool or sender state.

Fix: TXPOOL-ADMIT-BLOB-TRANSACTION
(src/application/services/txpool-blob-admission.lisp) runs, in order, the
known-transaction check and the stateless basics (TXPOOL-CHECK-BLOB-BASICS:
encoded size, the head fork's blob count limit, which bound the KZG work),
the sidecar shape and KZG verification outside the store guard, then the full
pool checks and the one atomic commit as before. A bad sidecar is now the
sender's fault whatever the pool would have answered.

Test: TXPOOL-BLOB-GOSSIP-DROPS-A-BAD-SIDECAR-THE-POOL-WOULD-REFUSE-ANYWAY
(tests/txpool-blob-admission-tests.lisp, integration): through a devnet
node's production backend, pool a blob transaction, then offer another with
the same sender, nonce and fees. Control: with a verifying proof it is an
ordinary refusal, 0 accepted and no error. With a proof that does not verify,
ETH-PEER-PROTOCOL-ERROR is signalled; the first transaction stays pooled and
the second leaves no blob behind.
