================================================================================
2. Classification, with the evidence for each
================================================================================

(a) ECIES tag does not authenticate the message -- 12,398, THEIRS.

The initiator encrypted its auth to a key other than ours. ECIES decryption
with the right key and the exact two prefix bytes as shared data is
deterministic; the only inputs are the recipient key and the bytes read.
Checked against the oracle:
  - geth v1.17.4 p2p/rlpx/rlpx.go (fetched at tag v1.17.4, byte-identical
    to references/go-ethereum 38271784) handshakeState.readMsg: 2-byte size,
    reject > 2048, read exactly size, ecies Decrypt(packet, nil, prefix).
    runRecipient -> readMsg -> handleAuthMsg -> makeAuthResp -> sealEIP8.
  - EIP-8 Auth2 and Auth3 (version 56, three extra elements) read whole and
    decode; Ack3 (version 57) opens; recovered ephemeral key, nonce and
    signature match (RLPX-HANDSHAKE-READER-TAKES-GETH-V1-17-4-EIP8-VECTORS-
    WHOLE). The existing RLPX-RECIPIENT-HANDSHAKE-MATCHES-EIP8-VECTORS still
    derives geth's pinned aes/mac secrets.
  - Auth2 opened with key A (not the addressee), or with the prefix omitted
    or one prefix byte flipped, fails with exactly this message; key B opens
    it (RLPX-AUTH-FOR-ANOTHER-NODE-FAILS-THE-ECIES-TAG).
  - 8 threads x 40 fresh auths (random initiator and ephemeral keys) all
    open, zero failures, with a misaddressed-auth positive control that the
    harness does catch (RLPX-RECIPIENT-OPENS-EVERY-FRESH-AUTH-ACROSS-
    THREADS). So no per-call or cross-thread state in ECDH, KDF, HMAC, AES.
  - Hive devp2p at d203fee6 (docs/evidence/sec5-d203fee6-hive-devp2p.txt):
    eth 25/25; those tests dial the client under test with geth's rlpx.Conn.
Whom they meant (inference, not verified): the Hoodi host has run at least
17 per-revision datadirs (datadir-<rev> in docs/evidence), each creating a
fresh nodekey, all advertised at 165.154.224.110:30303. Peers keep dialing
identities they learned earlier; the steady per-host retry pattern fits.
Operational remedy, not a code change: reuse one --nodekey across revisions.

(b) RLPx stream ended after 0 of 32 bytes -- 721; ~715 OURS.

32 bytes is the first frame header (2 x 16) after the handshake: we sent the
ack (and our Hello) and the peer closed without a frame. Inbound admission
built its eth Status from DEVNET-PEER-SYNC-STATUS, which takes the store
guard, BEFORE running rlpx-accept-stream, so the ack waited behind any hold.
geth's initiator allows 5 s for the whole encryption handshake
(transport.go handshakeTimeout, SetDeadline in doEncHandshake) and closes
silently on its own error (rlpxTransport.close sends Disconnect only for a
DiscReason). 715 of the 721 fall in 00:00-01:59Z, the window of 10-134 s
guard holds; 6 fell in the two hours before (baseline; a peer that hangs up
after the ack for its own reasons -- the slot test reproduces exactly that
line with a client that closes after reading our ack).
Reproduced: DEVNET-INBOUND-HANDSHAKE-DOES-NOT-WAIT-FOR-THE-STORE-GUARD holds
the server's guard from another thread and dials with a 10 s read bound. On
a07c912e the client got no ack (typep eth-peer failed); fixed, the whole
RLPx + Hello + Status handshake completes and the peer is admitted while
the guard is still held.

(c) 305 of 1024..1279 bytes -- 136, THEIRS (parity).

A pre-EIP-8 auth is 307 bytes with no prefix and begins 0x04; its first two
bytes read as a size of 0x04xx. EIP-8's Auth1 vector gives exactly
"305 of 1164" here (RLPX-LEGACY-PRE-EIP8-AUTH-IS-REFUSED-AS-GETH-V1-17-4-
REFUSES-IT). geth v1.17.4 has no legacy path (the EIP-8 pseudocode that tries
307 bytes first is gone from readMsg) and reads the same way, so these peers
fail against geth too. Given the bytes it asked for, the size-prefixed read
cannot misparse one either: the "key tag" it sees is 0x8c.

(d) listener.rejected "no-slot" -- 4,888, OURS, three defects.

  1. The log said "no-slot" for every non-:RESERVE verdict, including
     :IP-THROTTLED, :SUBNET-THROTTLED and :NETRESTRICT, so the live count
     cannot be split. Now it logs the verdict and the peers/pending counts
     it was taken on (DEVNET-LISTENER-REJECTION-NAMES-ITS-VERDICT: a
     netrestricted loopback accept read "no-slot" on a07c912e).
  2. The handshake reservation was released only at session teardown, so a
     live inbound peer counted twice (entry + pending) against maxpeers + 4,
     and twice toward the per-IP limit of 3. geth's listenLoop returns its
     pending slot when SetupConn returns. DEVNET-INBOUND-SLOT-IS-FREED-BY-
     FAILED-SESSIONS-AND-KEPT-BY-A-LIVE-ONE: on a07c912e a connected peer
     showed count 1, pending 1; fixed, pending 0 while it stays.
  3. Sessions blocked behind the store guard (b) held their reservations.
     From 01:50Z on, with no guard hold ending (none logged after 01:50:17Z),
     every reservation stayed held: one session outcome in 35 minutes, every
     accept refused. That the refusals then were genuinely no-slot is an
     inference: the old log cannot tell (defect 1).
     Also: an accepted stream had no read bound after the first byte, so a
     partial or legacy auth held its reservation until the peer hung up
     (DEVNET-INBOUND-SESSION-THAT-STALLS-MID-AUTH-GIVES-BACK-ITS-SLOT: on
     a07c912e no session_failed within 6 s; fixed, it ends on the stream
     timeout, shortened to 1 s in the test, 30 s in production).
Slot release on failure paths was already correct (unwind-protect): the
misaddressed-auth, trickled-then-closed and stalled sessions all return to
count 0 / pending 0 in the tests, and the existing DEVNET-PEER-SESSION-
FAILURE-DOES-NOT-TAKE-THE-NODE-DOWN still passes.

(e) The rest -- theirs or unknown, not investigated here: foreign chains
(777 network mismatch, 1 fork-id), no eth (184), peer Disconnects (164,
142 of them too-many-peers), resets (77), 11 eth/69 Status arity, 9 codec
errors, 4 mid-frame truncations, and one TYPE-ERROR (-1 vs UNSIGNED-BYTE 62)
that is probably ours (section 6).

3. Fixes
================================================================================

6fa17eb7  src/protocol/p2p/connection.lisp: RLPX-READ-HANDSHAKE-PACKET
  refuses a declared size above +RLPX-HANDSHAKE-MAX-SIZE+ (2048) before
  reading, as geth's readMsg does ("message too big").

479c1e5b
  - peer-table.lisp DEVNET-NODE-PUBLISH-STATUS-VIEW: at every store-guard
    release, publish (:head-number :head-timestamp :genesis-hash :best-hash);
    types.lisp slot STATUS-VIEW. peer-sync.lisp DEVNET-PEER-PUBLISHED-SYNC-
    STATUS builds the inbound Status from it (falls back to the guard only
    before the first publication); DEVNET-PEER-SYNC-STATUS reads the same
    fields through DEVNET-NODE-READ-STATUS-HEAD. The dialer still takes the
    guard before it dials, where no remote deadline is running.
  - peer-manager.lisp DEVNET-PEER-RUN-SESSION: the reservation is released
    as soon as admission resolves; teardown releases it only if still held.
  - eth-sync/node.lisp ETH-SYNC-ACCEPT-PEER :STREAM-TIMEOUT-SECONDS, passed
    *DEVNET-INBOUND-SESSION-STREAM-TIMEOUT-SECONDS* (30, the dialer's
    +DEVNET-SESSION-STREAM-TIMEOUT-SECONDS+). Divergence kept: geth bounds
    the whole encryption handshake at 5 s; we bound each read at 30 s.
  - peer-manager.lisp listener: p2p.listener.rejected fields reason (the
    verdict), peers, pending.

What the next run's log will say: listener.rejected with its real reason
and counts; 0-of-32 back near its baseline unless a peer closes after our
ack for its own reasons; no admission stuck behind the guard. The ECIES and
