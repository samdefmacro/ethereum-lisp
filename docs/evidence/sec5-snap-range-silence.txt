================================================================================
2. Mechanism
================================================================================

The three target_stale lines come from the dependency pool's stale check
(DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-CALL, src/app/cli/devnet/dialer.lisp): a pooled
StorageRanges request answered state-unavailable, the CL-authorized successor
was far ahead, and the lane signalled SNAP-SYNC-HEAL-YIELDED. The storage lane
pushed a :yield event (SNAP-SYNC-MULTI-PUSH-YIELD), the coordinator in
SNAP-SYNC-IMPORT-STATE-MULTI re-signalled it, and its unwind set the runtime's
stopped flag and then called SB-THREAD:JOIN-THREAD, with no timeout and no
log line, on every account, dependency, ByteCodes and StorageRanges lane and
the storage committer.

Every runtime wait checks the stopped flag. The pool's wait does not know the
runtime exists. DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-ACQUIRE, when every eligible peer's
slot for a response type is busy, slept on that type's waitqueue with no
timeout, and three things could wake it:

  - DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-RELEASE, CONDITION-NOTIFY (one waiter);
  - DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-FAIL-AND-RELEASE, CONDITION-NOTIFY (one waiter);
  - DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-REGISTER and the session-close release,
    CONDITION-BROADCAST.

A state-unavailable failure retires the peer for this pivot: it changes the
eligibility of every waiter, but woke one. The woken lane re-evaluated, found
another eligible peer still busy and slept again, consuming the wake-up. When
the last eligible peer was retired, the one lane it woke found no candidate
and returned (the pool-exhausted error); every other waiting lane stayed
asleep, and no request was left in flight to release a slot. The only
remaining wake-up, REGISTER, is called by the range coordinator's
REFRESH-SOURCES -- the thread now blocked in JOIN-THREAD on those very lanes.
New peers connecting did not help: ACQUIRE reads the live peer set only when
it wakes.

On Hoodi that shape was exactly what an aged pivot produces: 15 sources, one
StorageRanges lane each, fewer peers still serving 3720005 than lanes, and in
one second three of the remaining ones refusing the root. Earlier rebases
(16:40, 16:54, 17:08) went through because the refusing request was not
racing a queue of pool waiters; the 17:24 one was.

A second, independent silence sat under it: the coordinator's join printed
nothing however long it waited, and the storage-root closure walk (bounded by
*SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-ROOT-CLOSURE-MAX-NODES*, 1,048,576) printed nothing until
it finished, 43,657 ms for its last attempt on a store under compaction.

The brief's first hypothesis, an O(trie) closure walk at completion, is not
what happened and is not the walk's shape: it descends only nodes no
range-derived :STORAGE proof covers. Measured control (section 4): a
20,000-slot contract imported in byte-capped pages has 26,864 trie nodes and
the closure visits 4,346 of them. No incremental-marking change was made; I1
is untouched.

================================================================================
3. Fix
================================================================================

src/app/cli/devnet/dialer.lisp

  - ACQUIRE never sleeps longer than +DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-RECHECK-SECONDS+
    (1 s) without re-reading the live peer set: a peer that connected while
    every lane waited is used, and an eligibility change whose wake-up another
    lane consumed is seen within a second.
  - FAIL-AND-RELEASE broadcasts instead of notifying: a retired or cooling
    peer changes eligibility for every waiter.
  - The first stale verdict is latched on the pool (STALE-P,
    DEVNET-SNAP-SOURCE-POOL-LATCH-STALE), which broadcasts to every waiter;
    from then on ACQUIRE answers no-candidate and POOL-CALL signals
    SNAP-SYNC-HEAL-YIELDED at once, without another request and without
    calling the stale predicate again (the live run logged target_stale
    three times for one event).
  - New events: peer.snap.generation_stop (pivot, liveThreads, joined,
    elapsedMs) and peer.snap.storage_closure_progress (pivot, nodesVisited,
    multiGets, levels, elapsedMs). Both carry no peer identity.

src/networking/snap-sync/client.lisp

  - SNAP-SYNC-JOIN-GENERATION-THREADS replaces the five unbounded join loops
    of SNAP-SYNC-IMPORT-STATE-MULTI's unwind. The join itself stays unbounded
    (a lane still running may publish a batch and is never abandoned), but
    every *SNAP-SYNC-GENERATION-STOP-REPORT-SECONDS* (10 s) it reports the
    lanes still alive through the new :ON-GENERATION-STOP callback, and once
    more when a slow join completes. A lane that died on an unhandled
    condition still signals at the join, as before.
  - SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-ROOT-CLOSURE-WALK takes :PROGRESS and reports a
    SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-CLOSURE-PROGRESS after a multi-get at most every
    *SNAP-SYNC-STORAGE-CLOSURE-PROGRESS-SECONDS* (10 s);
    SNAP-SYNC-PUBLISH-STORAGE-ROOT-CLOSURE passes it through and
    SNAP-SYNC-MULTI-FILL-STORAGE-ROOT routes it to the storage profile
    callback. What the walk visits and publishes is unchanged.

scripts/hoodi-live-gate.sh counts the two new events and matches event names
with their quotes, because peer.snap.storage_closure is a prefix of
peer.snap.storage_closure_progress.

Does a closure survive a pivot rebase (brief item 3)? Yes, and now pinned:
the StorageRanges cursor set is keyed by account hash and storage root, not
the state root, and the :STORAGE-ROOT proof by the storage root. A generation
rebased to another state root meets the same content-addressed contract with
a complete cursor set and answers :ALREADY-CLOSED without a walk or a request.
Only the owning account page is re-fetched, since its cursor had not advanced.

5. Not verified

- Live confirmation. The a18b84e2 container was only read. The next Hoodi
  run on a revision with this fix should show, at the next stale pivot,
  pivot_rebased within seconds of target_stale, and generation_stop lines
  only if a lane is slow.
- Which three threads were stuck. /proc syscall and stack were not
  readable; the identification as pool waiters rests on the thread start
  times, the code, and the deterministic reproduction, not on a stack.
- A dialer-level loopback reproduction of the whole stall (several real
  sessions losing the pivot while lanes queue in the pool). The pool tests
  reproduce the wake-up loss and the stale latch directly; the importer test
  reproduces the silent join.
- Why the closure walk on Hoodi ran at ~1,500 nodes per second (compaction
  after the 12 GB import is the likely cause) and why that contract needed
  66,470 unproved nodes. The walk is bounded and now reports; it did not
  cause this stall.
- The 40 session threads each spending ~2.2% of a core in do_poll while idle
  (about 0.9 core in total) is a separate cost, not investigated here.
